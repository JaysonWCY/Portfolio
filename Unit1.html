Discussion Topic: Project Failures Study

Please read the Agrawal et al. (2024) paper and then answer the following questions:

Question 1:

What do you believe are the three most common reasons for project failure?

Question 2:

Give two examples of failures that support your choices (there are several examples in the lecturecast).


RESPONSE

Discussion Topic: Project Failures Study

Based on (Agrawal et al., 2024), some of the common reasons of project failure are due to the lack of knowledge, processing error, as well as issues that come with having an organisation monitoring the project.

Overlooking a design element involves issues from not knowing full structures to issues of missing details that could end up being crucial to the process.

Errors from not following proper design are errors that occur when specific situations that require complicated responses arise. This requires components not in the original design, which in the long term can cause many issues and complications. This error is similar to overlooking a design element, as an experienced team would have included the case that creates this issue in the original design.

Having an organisation with a fixed mindset could result in higher pressure and stress to perform, hence lowering quality. Opinions of having multiple higher-ups providing different opinions and instructions would also cause friction and complications in designs.

Examples of failures of such would be the failures from risks. All three issues above can lead to the unnecessary up-taking of risks, either from not knowing the risk or being pressured to do the task even with the risk.

Another example would be failure from estimation and analysis. This would follow the same logic as before, as a lack of knowledge or expertise along with pressure to ignore standard estimation and analysis procedures to meet expectations of the organisation could lead to failure in estimation and analysis.

The Tacoma Narrows Bridge (1940) (Ammann et al., n.d.) incident is an example of overlooking a design. The engineers had forgotten to account for the winds as well as the pedestrians’ footsteps causing oscillations which caused the bridge to collapse.

NASA’s Challenger Disaster (1986) (Hall, 2003) resulted in the disintegration of the space shuttle causing the death of seven crew members. The cause of which was due to pressure caused by stakeholders to keep on launch schedules even though the engineers were concerned about certain aspects of the shuttle failing on the day of the launch.

While my peers discussed a range of issues and assigned varying levels of importance to them, there was a clear consensus around the core concepts presented in the article by Agrawal et al. (2024). Each person brought up different case studies and highlighted unique aspects of each incident, but all aligned with the overarching classification of errors outlined in the article. These categories include cognitive, knowledge-based, organizational, and process-related errors. Notably, no one disputed the idea that most project failures can be traced back to one or more of these key categories, reinforcing the relevance and applicability of the framework.
