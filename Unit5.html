Unit 5

User Experience

Discussion Topic: Scanning Exercise and Results

Human emotions can affect the user experience, a fact which contributes to the complexity of user satisfaction with a product. Further complicating the process is the fact that user emotions on the first use of a product are likely to be different from their emotions once they become more experienced.

Read Gu et al. (2023) and discuss whether you agree with the authors that their findings can improve the accuracy of subjective evaluation.

RESPONSE

After reading the article regarding the halo effect on digital interfaces (Gu et al., 2023), I completely agree with the hypothesis and findings as well as how it can improve the accuracy of subjective gradings. The interface of a product is the main component that interacts with the user itself. A well-received interface may trigger positive emotions which then will be carried into the evaluations which in turn could inflate the scores. While over time the strength of this effect can fade, usability and performance were still rated higher, which means that a nice interface could possibly hide usability or performance issues. This is the same effect as having a page freeze while it loads as opposed to having a loading bar. If both are loading for the same time, a loading bar would be perceived as the software doing its job while the page freezing could be mistaken for software failure.

Overall, the article discusses how a customer might rate a new product higher than its actual value if the interface is nice, but later on will provide a more balanced and functionality-focused review. This can be important when gathering information about the aesthetics of a product as opposed to gathering information about the performance of a product.

After reading the discussions from my other peers, it seems all are in agreement that the halo effect is an important aspect to be considered in both performance and aesthetics. They all agree that review of the aesthetics should be left for a few days after the initial interaction. The importance of timing has become a highlight in evaluations of aesthetics. The question of how to properly test usability and importance then comes in, as it seems that the halo effect tends to stay longer with these aspects. Maybe the possibility of making a neutral interface to test the performance and usability. These all have to come together for the awareness of the effect that could skew performance and aesthetic reviews.
